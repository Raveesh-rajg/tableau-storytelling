# Analytical writeup — The Great Convergence

**Question.** Did the world's health outcomes converge in the second half
of the 20th century — and for whom did convergence fail?

**Data.** Gapminder: 142 countries, every 5 years 1952-2007; life
expectancy, population, GDP per capita. Known limitations: national
averages hide within-country inequality; 5-year sampling smooths shocks
(the 1994 Rwandan genocide appears only as the 1992-1997 dip); the panel
excludes several small states.

**Findings (all computed from the file).**
1. Median national life expectancy rose 45.1 -> 71.9 years, 1952 -> 2007.
2. Weighted by population, the share of humanity in countries with life
   expectancy >= 70 went from 1.1% to 61.4%.
3. The largest gains were middle-income, not rich: Oman +38.1 years,
   Vietnam +33.8, Indonesia +33.2.
4. Convergence reversed in southern Africa: between 1987 and 2007,
   Zimbabwe lost 18.9 years, Eswatini 18.1, Lesotho 14.6, Botswana 12.9 —
   the demographic signature of the HIV/AIDS epidemic.

**Why a story, not a dashboard.** The four findings order themselves:
baseline, mechanism, winners, exception. A dashboard invites readers to
miss finding 4; the story format makes the exception unskippable, which
is the ethical requirement here.
